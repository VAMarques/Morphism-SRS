<h2>1 Stokes proof</h2>
Sea $F = (P(x,y,z), Q(x,y,z), R(x,y,z)) $ y sea $S$ una superficie parametrizada por $f:D \subseteq \mathbb R^2 \to S \subseteq \mathbb R^3$ suave.

<hr>
Si
\[
f(u,v)=
\bigl(x(u,v),y(u,v),z(u,v)\bigr).
\]

Entonces

\[
Df_{(u,v)} =
\begin{pmatrix}
x_u & x_v\\
y_u & y_v\\
z_u & z_v
\end{pmatrix}
\]
Luego
\[
\mathbf F\circ f
=

(P\circ f,Q\circ f,R\circ f)
\]

Podemos considerar:
\[
\d\mathbf r
 =
Df
\begin{pmatrix}
du\\
dv
\end{pmatrix}
\]

Luego

\[
\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r
=

(\mathbf F\circ f)
Df
\begin{pmatrix}
du\\
dv
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Sean
\[
(A,B)
=

(\mathbf F\circ f)Df.
\]

La integral se vuelve:
\[
\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf F\cdot \d\mathbf r
=

\oint_{\partial S}
A\,\d u+B\,\d v = \iint_S
\left(
\frac{\partial B}{\partial u}
-
\frac{\partial A}{\partial v}
\right)
\,\d u\wedge\d v
\]

Solo necesitamos entonces:

\[
\mathrm D_u B - \mathrm D_v A
\]
<hr>
Tenemos$$
A = \sum_{i=1}^3 (F \circ f)_{(i)}\mathrm D_1f^{(i)}
$$

$$
\frac{\partial A}{\partial v} = \sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{\partial (F \circ f)^{(i)}}{\partial v}\mathrm D_1f^{(i)} + (F \circ f)_{(i)}\mathrm D_{2,1}f^{(i)}
$$
Del mismo modo$$
B = \sum_{i=1}^3 (F \circ f)_{(i)}\mathrm D_2f^{(i)}
$$
Entonces$$
\frac{\partial B}{\partial u} = \sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{\partial (F \circ f)^{(i)}}{\partial u}\mathrm D_2f^{(i)} + (F \circ f)_{(i)}\mathrm D_{1,2}f^{(i)}
$$
Luego\begin{align}
\frac{\partial B}{\partial u} - \frac{\partial A}{\partial v}
&= \sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{\partial (F \circ f)^{(i)}}{\partial u}\mathrm D_2f^{(i)} -
\frac{\partial (F \circ f)^{(i)}}{\partial v}\mathrm D_1f^{(i)} \\
&= \sum_{i=1}^3 \sum_{j=1}^3 \frac{\partial F^{(i)}}{\partial f^{(j)}}\mathrm D_1f^{(j)}\mathrm D_2f^{(i)} -
\sum_{i=1}^3 \sum_{j=1}^3 \frac{\partial F^{(i)}}{\partial f^{(j)}}\mathrm D_2f^{(j)}\mathrm D_1f^{(i)} \\
&= D_2f_{(u,v)} \mathrm D F_{f(u,v)} D_1 f_{(u,v)} -  D_1f_{(u,v)} \mathrm D F_{f(u,v)}  D_2 f_{(u,v)}
\end{align}
<hr>
$\vec a^{\top}A\vec b = \sum_{i,j} a_i A_{ij}b_j$,
Luego\begin{align}
\vec a^{\top}A\vec b - \vec b^{\top}A\vec a &= \sum_{i,j} A_{ij}(a_ib_j - a_jb_i) \\
&= \sum_{1\le i< j \le 3} (A_{ij}-A_{ji})(a_ib_j - a_jb_i) \\
&= \begin{pmatrix} A_{23} - A_{32} \\ A_{31} - A_{13} \\ A_{12} - A_{21}\end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} a_2b_3 - a_3b_2 \\ a_3b_1 - a_1b_3 \\ a_1b_2 - a_2b_1 \end{pmatrix} \\
&= 2\operatorname{Alt}(A)(\vec a, \vec b) = \operatorname{Axl} A \cdot \vec b \times \vec a
\end{align}
(Axl depende de la orientación elegida). Para nuestro caso, tenemos que, si $F=(P,Q,R)$ Entonces:$$
\operatorname{Axl}(\mathrm D F_{f(u,v)}) = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{\partial R}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial Q}{\partial z} \\ \frac{\partial P}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial R}{\partial x} \\ \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\end{pmatrix} = \nabla \times F_{f(u,v)}
$$
Asi que:
$$
D_2f_{(u,v)} \mathrm D F_{f(u,v)} D_1 f_{(u,v)} -  D_1f_{(u,v)} \mathrm D F_{f(u,v)}  D_2 f_{(u,v)} = \nabla \times F_{f(u,v)} \cdot D_1 f_{(u,v)} \times D_2f_{(u,v)}
$$
Entonces finalmente concluimos:\[
\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf F\cdot \d\mathbf r
=
\iint_S
\nabla \times F_{f(u,v)} \cdot D_1 f_{(u,v)} \times D_2f_{(u,v)}
\,\d u\wedge\d v
\]
Pero $\d \mathbf S = D_1 f_{(u,v)} \times D_2f_{(u,v)}\,\d u\wedge\d v$, tenemos:\[
\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf F\cdot \d\mathbf r
=
\iint_S
\nabla \times F \cdot \d \mathbf S
\]
